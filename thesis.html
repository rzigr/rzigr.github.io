Computational analysis of linguistic agency

Igor Ryazanov, Umeå University, Department of Computing Science

On this page you can find a tentative summary of my upcoming PhD thesis (to be defended in Autumn 2026). If you'd like to know more about my other activities, check the home page.

In my thesis, I apply NLP methods to computational analysis of media narratives through detecting and investigating tropes and semantic structures specific to these narratives, such as the notion of 'threat' in Papers 1 and 2. In my subsequent work, I focus on agency, which has been shown to play an important role in a variety of media narratives. I analyse agency as part of the narratives around AI technology and its anthropomorphism (Papers 2 and 5), and the coverage of casualties in armed conflicts (Paper 4). Paper 3 aims to investigate how readers perceive agency in the news headlines and attempts to quantify it as a target for a computational task. Paper 5 is an ongoing project that investigates AI narratives on social media and how AI expert annotations can help their computational analysis. Additionally, the introductory chapter and Paper 3 are partially based on the peer-reviewed thesis proposal (listed in 'Other publications' below). These are the broad research questions I am addressing:

  • RQ1 How can linguistic agency be analysed, modelled and predicted computationally?
  • RQ2 What role does linguistic agency play in media narratives?

Papers to be included in the thesis

  1. How Does the Language of ‘Threat’ Vary Across News Domains? A Semi-Supervised Pipeline for Understanding Narrative Components in News Contexts.
    Authors: Ryazanov, I. & Björklund, J.
    Status: Published in Proceedings of the 35th Annual Workshop of the Swedish Artificial Intelligence Society, 94-99, 2023

    Abstract: By identifying and characterising the narratives told in news media we can better understand political and societal processes. The problem is challenging from the perspective of natural language processing because it requires a combination of quantitative and qualitative methods. This paper reports on work in progress, which aims to build a human-in-the-loop pipeline for analysing how the variation of narrative themes across different domains, based on topic modelling and word embeddings. As an illustration, we study the language associated with the threat narrative in British news media.

  2. How ChatGPT changed the media’s narratives on AI: a semi-automated narrative analysis through frame semantics.
    Authors: Ryazanov, I., Öhman, C., & Björklund, J.
    Status: Published in Minds and Machines, 35(1), 1-24, 2024.

    Abstract: We perform a mixed-method frame semantics-based analysis on a dataset of more than 49,000 sentences collected from 5846 news articles that mention AI. The dataset covers the twelve-month period centred around the launch of OpenAI’s chatbot ChatGPT and is collected from the most visited open-access English-language news publishers. Our findings indicate that during the six months succeeding the launch, media attention rose tenfold—from already historically high levels. During this period, discourse has become increasingly centred around experts and political leaders, and AI has become more closely associated with dangers and risks. A deeper review of the data also suggests a qualitative shift in the types of threat AI is thought to represent, as well as the anthropomorphic qualities ascribed to it.

  3. Perceived Agency in News Headlines: A Computational Perspective on News Framing
    Authors:Ryazanov, I., Hardmeier, C. & Björklund, J.
    Status: Under revision. (Previously submitted to ARR, 2025)

    Abstract: Agency attribution is a powerful narrative device, particularly influential in news framing. Perceived agency and its manipulation through linguistic means is a key component of many highly salient narrative devices, such as the dehumanisation of minoritised groups and the anthropomorphism of technology. We focus on linguistically communicated agency as its own computational task, focusing on perceived agency --- examining how agency is interpreted from the reader's perspective. To support this, we introduce a novel, manually annotated dataset of perceived agency in English-language news headlines, and analyse the respondents' agreement. We further discuss several approaches to the prediction task, including regression of numerical values, token classification, and prompt-based annotation via generative LLMs.

  4. Ukrainians Killed by Russia; Palestinians Dead? A Computational Semantic Analysis of Western Media’s Attribution of Agency in War
    Authors: Ryazanov, I. & Öhman, C.
    Status: In preparation for submission; Preregistration available here.

    Abstract To whom or what does Western media attribute casualties in war? Focusing on Gaza and Ukraine as high-profile cases, we assess 34,608 articles collected from 7 large media outlets. Drawing on quantitative semantic analysis, the study identifies whether reports on casualties include a causing subject/agent, and, if so, the degree to which subjects are animate or inanimate agents (e.g., “soldier” or “bullet”). We highlight the implications of these patterns for the academic study of war coverage, as well as the popular debate on media bias.

Ongoing Study (to be included tentatively)

  1. AI experts meet AI narratives on BlueSky
    Authors: Ryazanov, I., Siglidis, I., Björklund, J. & Antoniak, M.
    Status: Data collection

    Abstract: Narrative structures are notoriously difficult to analyse computationally. In our work, we aim to isolate 'master narratives' within online discourse on AI. To this end, we host a series of workshops with AI experts from various academic fields, and collect open-text annotations of AI narratives present on BlueSky. We analyse the narratives and compare them to the output of LLM-based labelling tools. We further consider a combination of expert and generated annotations to improve future crowdsourced studies of AI narratives.

Other publications

  • Thesis Proposal: Detecting Agency Attribution.
    Authors:Ryazanov, I. & Björklund, J.
    Status: Published in Proceedings of the 18th Conference of the European Chapter of the Association for Computational Linguistics: Student Research Workshop, 208-214, 2024.

    Abstract: We explore computational methods for perceived agency attribution in natural language data. We consider ‘agency’as the freedom and capacity to act, and the corresponding Natural Language Processing (NLP) task involves automatically detecting attributions of agency to entities in text. Our theoretical framework draws on semantic frame analysis, role labelling and related techniques. In initial experiments, we focus on the perceived agency of AI systems. To achieve this, we analyse a dataset of English-language news coverage of AI-related topics, published within one year surrounding the release of the Large Language Model-based service ChatGPT, a milestone in the general public’s awareness of AI. Building on this, we propose a schema to annotate a dataset for agency attribution and formulate additional research questions to answer by applying NLP models.

  • Developing a Multilingual Corpus of Wikipedia Biographies.
    Authors: Devinney, H., Eklund, A., Ryazanov, I., & Cai, J. (2023).
    Status: Published In Proceedings of the 14th International Conference on Recent Advances in Natural Language Processing, 2023

    Abstract: For many languages, Wikipedia is the most accessible source of biographical information. Studying how Wikipedia describes the lives of people can provide insights into societal biases, as well as cultural differences more generally. We present a method for extracting datasets of Wikipedia biographies. The accompanying codebase is adapted to English, Swedish, Russian, Chinese, and Farsi, and is extendable to other languages. We present an exploratory analysis of biographical topics and gendered patterns in four languages using topic modelling and embedding clustering. We find similarities across languages in the types of categories present, with the distribution of biographies concentrated in the language’s core regions. Masculine terms are over-represented and spread out over a wide variety of topics. Feminine terms are less frequent and linked to more constrained topics. Non-binary terms are nearly non-represented.

  • Programmatic advertising in the age of AI: a conceptual overview and strategic recommendations
    Authors: Åbonde Garke, A., Wahlund, R., Leckner, S., Häglund, E., Cai, J., Ryazanov, I., & Björklund, J.
    Status: Submitted to ....

    Abstract: Artificial intelligence (AI) is revolutionizing the field of programmatic digital marketing. This article provides a forward-looking perspective on AI in advertising and offers a conceptual framework for developing effective marketing strategies. We explore key developments in the industry: the progression from personalized to contextual targeting, and the increased reliance on AI-based automation. Additionally, the article identifies three critical factors – results, resources, and rectitude – that influence the choice of strategies for online advertisers. Our findings suggest that while AI might multiply the effectiveness of programmatic advertising campaigns, it comes with important tradeoffs that must be taken into account. By synthesizing literature from digital advertising, computer science, and media studies, we offer an improved understanding of the evolving programmatic advertising ecosystem and distill this into practical advice for advertisers.
