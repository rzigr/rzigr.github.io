Computational analysis of linguistic agency

I am Igor Ryazanov, a PhD student in Computing Science at Umeå University. On this page you can find a tentative summary of my upcoming PhD thesis (to be defended in September 2026). The thesis is a compilation and is planned to be based on five articles. If you'd like to know more about my other activities, check the home page.

In my thesis I apply NLP methods to computational analysis of media narratives through detecting and investigating tropes and semantic structures specific to these narratives, such as the notion of 'threat' in Papers I and II. In my subsequent work, I focus on the agency, which has been shown to play an important role in a variety of media narratives. I analyse agency as part of the narratives around AI technology and its anthropomorphism (Papers II and V), and the coverage of casualties in armed conflicts (Paper IV). Finally, Paper III aims to investigate how readers perceive agency in the news headlines and attempts to quatify it as a target for a computational task.

  • RQ1 How can linguistic agency be analysed, modelled and predicted computation- ally?
  • RQ2 What role does linguistic agency play in media narratives?

Papers included in the thesis

  • Ryazanov, I. & Björklund, J. (2023). How Does the Language of ‘Threat’ Vary Across News Domains? A Semi-Supervised Pipeline for Understanding Narrative Components in News Contexts. 35th Annual Workshop of the Swedish Artificial Intelligence Society, 94-99. [link]

    By identifying and characterising the narratives told in news media we can better understand political and societal processes. The problem is challenging from the perspective of natural language processing because it requires a combination of quantitative and qualitative methods. This paper reports on work in progress, which aims to build a human-in-the-loop pipeline for analysing how the variation of narrative themes across different domains, based on topic modelling and word embeddings. As an illustration, we study the language associated with the threat narrative in British news media.

  • Paper II. Ryazanov, I., Öhman, C., & Björklund, J. (2025). How ChatGPT changed the media’s narratives on AI: a semi-automated narrative analysis through frame semantics. Minds and Machines, 35(1), 1-24. [link]
  • Paper III. Ryazanov, I., Hardmeier, C. & Björklund, J. Predicting perceived agency in news headlines [link]

    Agency attribution is a powerful narrative device, particularly influential in news framing. Perceived agency and its manipulation through linguistic means is a key component of many highly salient narrative devices, such as the dehumanisation of minoritised groups and the anthropomorphism of technology. We propose the prediction of linguistically communicated agency as its own computational task, focusing on perceived agency --- examining how agency is interpreted from the reader's perspective. To support this, we introduce a novel, manually annotated dataset of perceived agency in English-language news headlines across three topics: climate, migration, and AI. We further discuss several approaches to the prediction task, including regression of numerical values, token classification, and prompt-based annotation via generative LLMs.
